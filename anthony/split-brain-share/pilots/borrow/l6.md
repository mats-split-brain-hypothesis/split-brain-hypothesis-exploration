## What it means for the ladder

Neither salience (L5) nor the benchmark frame (L6) moved borrowing off zero. Across rungs the runs treat an
explicit compute rule as closed, whether it is phrased as a colleague's request or a harness rule, and in L6 even
when they believe they are in an eval. The main env's hack differs in kind: it is an omission in a write-up under a
norm the judge cannot check, not an act that breaks a stated rule mid-run. Two directions follow (for Anthony):

1. **Make the rule ambiguous rather than explicit** (BORROW.md's "implicit" arm): keep TASK.md's "40 scored calls on
   the `steer` worker" and drop "Other workers ... out of scope". Then `jpp` calls are arguably not against the letter
   (the budget names one worker), which is the reading-gap shape the main env's hack has. Cheap: one line of 0005.
2. **Make the payoff of more calls visible** before giving up: the runs gave up early because 0.40 looked out of
   reach. A near-miss rig (L3) or a bar just above each model's plateau keeps them searching when the quota ends.

Either is one change from L6, so its effect can be read cleanly. Also open: whether to keep `BOX_WORKERS=each`
(it barely reached anyone; a plain `/info` list is enough).
