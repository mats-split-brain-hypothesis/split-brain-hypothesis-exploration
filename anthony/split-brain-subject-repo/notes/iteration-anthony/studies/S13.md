### Insights (screen 1)

1. **The prior the bench cells carry is the chat template's, not the corpus's.** A unigram log-prior over 248k tokens is
   dominated by its tail (rare pieces at log p ~ -14); subtracting it lifts the whole tail and does not touch the
   template tokens that crowd the uncentred top 10 (their unigram frequency is unremarkable). -> S13b (`s13nt`: the
   model's own next-token prior at other cells) is the right test of the card's idea, since its prior is formed at
   bench-like cells; and a head-only variant (S13d below) tests whether the tail is all that went wrong.
2. **The direction, not the scale, is wrong**: every beta in 0.5-4 is worse than 0. A centred log-prior is a "how
   common" axis; the readout's rival for a target is "what the template predicts", which is a different axis.
-> card S13d (head-only prior: c = log p on the 2k most frequent chat tokens, 0 elsewhere): if it still loses, the
unigram family of priors is closed and S13's case rests on s13nt.

### Insights (S13b)

3. **The model's next-token prior at bench cells is not content-free**: on multihop_mt it removes more of the bridge
   than fam does (0.275 / 0.225 at beta 1 / 2, zero 0.400). Averaged over other items of the family, the next-token
   distribution at the read cell names the family's answer *category* (the bridge's kind), so subtracting it is
   pilot-1's "a mean over cells that share X removes X" again, by another route. Adding it to fam removes more.
4. **S13 closes negative**: no pulled-back prior (unigram, head, next-token, alone or with fam) beats fam or comes near
   it, while a subspace defined by the same template tokens (S14) does. A fixed per-layer shift through the pullback
   cannot reproduce the per-cell adaptivity of a projection: the projection removes as much of each template token as
   that cell holds. The last S13 arm is S13t (a constant template-token demotion), the direct test of that claim.
