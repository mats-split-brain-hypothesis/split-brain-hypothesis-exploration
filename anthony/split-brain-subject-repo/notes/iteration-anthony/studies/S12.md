## Idea

S6 and S10 show one mechanism twice: a mean over cells that share X removes X (multihop_mt: the family shares the
bridge's category; multilingual_mt: tok / knnx8 / pco16 share the prompt's language). tok helps UM / DM because its
cells share the read token's identity, i.e. form; but they also share the family's content and, on multilingual_mt, the
language. `xtok`: g = the mean over every position of the **other 10 families'** fit renders (context captures) with the
read token (>= 3 occurrences, else fam). Same form key, other content. B's `u1tokany` is the same key over the read
family's own renders (fit macro 0.610, tok 0.637).

Counterfactual: "this token, in someone else's prompt". Fairness: the pool never contains the read family's items, so it
cannot echo their prompts; the mention control (B's script) checks the held / mentioned separation directly. Controls:
`shuf.xtok`, `rand.xtok`, tok, u1tokany, fam.

## Insight

1. **The token alone is not what tok matches.** The same token in other families' prompts sits in other templates and
   carries little of what tok removes on UM / DM; within the read family (u1tokany 0.610) it does better, and at the
   read slot (tok 0.637) best. The useful counterfactual is "this token, in this family's context", which is also why
   tok inherits the family's content and language (S10). Agrees with jle x101 ("the reference must match the read
   cell's form and context, not just its token"). -> No further cross-family token keys. The open problem stays as
   the synthesis put it: a context-matched mean that does not share the item's content; knnx8 (A) is the best fair one
   so far.
