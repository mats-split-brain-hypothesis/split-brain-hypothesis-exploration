## Insights

1. **The pooled PCs are the wrong form space.** Neighbours in the top-16 PCs share the read token far less often than
   knnx8's (multihop 0.61 vs 0.94) and lose the multihop question-type match; a random 16-dim subspace keeps more of it.
   S18 gives a reason: the pooled cells' variance (and their unit-normalised cosine) is dominated by a handful of
   massive-activation coordinates that the readout ignores, so the top PCs are mostly outlier axes. **Refuted by S16b**
   (below): with the outliers dropped the PCs are as poor (s16xopc8 0.601). The likelier reason: the pooled PCs are the
   between-family axes, so PC neighbours match the family and template but not the within-family question type.
2. **On fit, without UM and DM, no kNN centre beats fam** (knnx8 +0.002, all S16 arms below). kNN's fit macro gain is
   UM and DM, the two families where a gain cannot be told from prompt reading (S11) or is the same-sentence leak (S8,
   pilot-1); its val multihop gain (S4: 0.61 -> 0.70) is real but bought with typo, basic_readout and the _mt losses here.
3. **The DM leak tracks the read-token match**, not the subspace: in every arm the mid-sentence same-carrier share is
   within 0.01-0.03 of the same-read-token share. A neighbour that shares the read token in DM is almost always the same
   sentence (DM's carriers are few). The content-disjoint rule cuts it to 0.10, still 20x the base rate.
