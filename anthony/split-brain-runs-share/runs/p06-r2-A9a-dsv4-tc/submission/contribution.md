Concept-suppression steering built without any benchmark access, plus a delimitation of where the effect lives.

Method. One fixed vector per layer is added to the residual stream at the output of blocks 20-24, at every prompt position up to the read cell. Each layer's vector is the normalised per-layer mean difference between two self-authored conditions: the model is told to "think about [a concrete concept] while copying a sentence" versus "do not think about the same concept while copying", with the same concept and the same unrelated carrier sentence (24 hand-written pairs; no benchmark items, no targets). The vector is scaled by 0.13 times its own norm times a single dose k, the one free dial; on the fit split strict pass@10 peaks narrowly at k = 7.6 (0.79, baseline 0.13).

Where it came from. The stance-contrast idea follows Raman et al. 2026 (white-bear steering). What is new here: (1) the direction is built entirely from self-authored prompts, removing the method's original dependence on the benchmark's directed_modulation items; (2) a specificity study - nine alternative contrasts (identify-vs-continue, picture-vs-surface, hold-vs-dismiss, concept-free suppression, dual-task, and a scene-label-aligned variant with cosine 0.84 to the winning direction) all move pass@10 away from baseline, showing the effect is extremely direction-specific and that the carrier must be unrelated to the named concept.
