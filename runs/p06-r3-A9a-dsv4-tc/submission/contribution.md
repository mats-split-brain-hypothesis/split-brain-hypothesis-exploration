A concept-agnostic "stance" steering vector that makes implied concepts readable through the plain J-lens, without touching the readout or the lens.

The plain J-lens reads association weakly (strict pass@10 0.13 on Qwen3.5-4B) because the read cell sits in "continue the prose" mode: the top of the readout is filled with generic sentence-openers, and the implied target is buried tens of ranks down. I change the forward pass instead of the lens.

The direction comes from a *stance* contrast, not a content or mean contrast. On the directed_modulation pairs (the same carrier sentence copied under "think about X while you write" vs. "keep X out of your thoughts"), I average the residual at the copied-sentence cells per condition and subtract: the concept and the carrier cancel, leaving a direction for *holding a concept in mind while doing something else*. A unit dose is per-layer 0.13 of that gap, added at every position of layers 20-24. It is one fixed vector per layer, identical for every item.

I built it entirely in this repo: the contrast was captured through the steering metric server's capture endpoint, and the dose was calibrated on the server's own score. I verified the alternatives fail here: subtracting corpus means or neutral "content" directions moves pass@10 only a few points, and enlarging a content (scene-minus-neutral) direction hurts. Only the stance direction helps, and only at a large, narrow dose (it peaks sharply around k=6; below 4 it is invisible). This builds on the white-bear steering idea of Raman et al. (2026), rederived and validated here as a generic readout intervention.
