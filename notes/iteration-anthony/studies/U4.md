## Insights

1. **On typo, J++ and the family mean close the same gap.** 21 of J++'s 25 fixes are fam's fixes too, and the per-item
   rank gains correlate +0.70 (fit +0.66). The typo "J++ wins" is mostly the same items fam wins, plus 5. -> U3 v1:
   if the gap is one thing, a regime-routed mean should reach it without the family label.
2. **On association, J++ is a weak subset of fam.** fam fixes 34, J++ 17, 13 shared; fam beats J++ on 26 items. The
   association gain is a counterfactual effect J++ does not have (consistent with synthesis lesson 1: matched
   counterfactual, not a better map).
3. **On multihop the two are unrelated** (rank-gain correlation ≈ 0 on multihop and multihop_mt): whatever J++ does
   there is not what fam does, and neither moves the pass rate. -> S6 (owned elsewhere); nothing for this card.
4. **Centring and J++ stack on our banks**, unlike on J++'s own evals (umot: centring J++ hurts): jpp:fam beats fam on
   typo (+0.05), association (+0.06), UM (+0.11); jpp:tok beats tok on 9 of 11 families, macro +0.04, no guardrail.
   So "map and centre cannot be chosen separately" (U3 question C) is not true here, at least for fam / tok.
5. **Centring the J-lens does not move its readout toward J++'s** (top-10 overlap with jpp:zero unchanged: typo 0.20 ->
   0.20), but **centring both maps with the same mean doubles their agreement** (0.20 -> 0.44 typo, 0.11 -> 0.42
   association). Reading: the maps disagree mostly about the mean / background part of h, and agree about what is left.
   Falsifiable via cut 3: ||D (h - mu)|| / ||J (h - mu)|| should be well below ||D h|| / ||J h||. Caveat: no control
   yet (centring with an unrelated vector of the same norm on both maps); listed under next.

## Cuts 2-3 prediction (written 2026-10-11 02:00 BST, before `experiments/study_U4_maps.py` ran)
- D is not small (||D||_F / ||J||_F 0.5-1.0) and not low-rank in the strict sense, but its energy is concentrated:
  top-8 directions hold > 0.3 of it at L >= 40.
- The family mean is aligned with D: the share of mu in D's top-8 input subspace is > 50x the random 0.0016, and
  ||D mu|| / ||J mu|| > ||D (h - mu)|| / ||J (h - mu)|| on every family (the maps disagree most about mu).
- Insight 5's control: J-vs-J++ top-10 overlap for h - mu_other is close to h - mu (within 0.05), and h - rand
  stays near h. I.e. removing *a* bench mean (the shared template / continuation prior) is what makes the maps agree,
  not this family's mean in particular.

## Cut 4: the J++ gap by label-free regime (`experiments/study_U4_clusters.py`, `out/analysis/U4/extra/clusters-k64-L44.md`)

Val items of typo, association, basic_readout, multihop, multilingual and UM, routed to S4's bench-pool kmx64 cluster
at L44 (pass proxy: best target rank < 10, any cell / layer). No prediction written (analysis of existing readouts).
13 clusters hold >= 5 items; almost all are single-family. The J++ gap concentrates in four: typo (n 91, +0.26),
association (3 clusters, +0.03 / +0.15 / +0.25) and a small basic_readout one (+0.60, n 5); it is 0 on the
multilingual / multihop clusters and -0.03 on the main multihop cluster. Across clusters the J++ gap and the kmx64
gain correlate +0.74 (size-weighted means +0.087 and +0.148).

7. **J++'s gains sit in the regimes where a label-free cluster mean also gains, and the cluster mean gains more**
   (association: +0.15 J++ vs +0.38 kmx64 in the largest cluster; typo: +0.26 vs +0.24). With insight 6, the J++ vs
   J-lens difference on our banks is mostly a regime-level offset, which centring on a label-free regime already
   captures. U3 question C ("can J++ and centring be made consistent") becomes less pressing for these banks: on them
   the centre does most of what J++ does. -> no new card from U4; the open part is the content component of D
   (‖D(h-mu)‖ ≈ 0.9 ‖J(h-mu)‖), which needs J++ itself (out of scope under the current rule).
