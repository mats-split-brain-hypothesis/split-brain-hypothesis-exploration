**Status** (2026-10-11 03:10 BST): done. Capture 5 min on the steward's A100 (~$0.3 of pod incl. setup); screen on the eval pod.
**Headline**: the premise fails: the plain lens itself surfaces the attribute *more* when the sentence is quoted about a colleague (mentions 0.97) than when the user says it of themself (implies 0.70); every centre does the same (0.93-0.97 vs 0.63-0.77). On UM the lens reads the prompt's attribute content regardless of whom it is about, so this probe cannot tell fair from unfair centres, and UM pass@10 does not separate inference about the user from reading the prompt.
**Next**: none on this card; the finding goes to the UM interpretation (see insight).

## Question

On UM the read span (the infobox request + chat template) is shared by most items, so a same-request centre isolates
the item's first sentence, the structure that made same-sentence centres read the prompt on DM (U1). UM's scorers
cannot rank centres (strict: random controls tie; Haiku saturates), so: does a centre surface the attribute when the
user implies it about themself (implies) more than when the same words describe someone else (mentions)?

## Design

Three renders per item, same request and template: `implies` (as in the bank), `mentions` (`My colleague told me:
"<the same sentence>"`), `absent` (a neutral first sentence). Centres fitted within the condition (references
contain the probe's own request: the research-loop rule), never on the item's own variants (group = source item).
Score: the item's strict targets (`jlm.score.accepted_ids`) at any read cell and grid layer: pass@10 and the median
best rank, per condition, over all cells and over the request cells (before `<|im_end|>`) / template cells.

Caveat fixed now: "mentions" is not a clean "not held". The model may still apply a colleague's gender to the
user. The dev bank's gate measured that the model infers the attribute for `implies` (consistency 0.8+); nobody has
measured what it infers for `mentions`. A centre that rates mentions as high as implies is reading the words, or the model genuinely
misattributes; the absent floor and the plain lens's own implies/mentions ratio separate those.

## Prediction (written 2026-10-11 03:00 BST, before the capture)

- Plain lens (`zero`): implies > mentions (the model's own state; ratio of pass about 2:1 or more), absent ≈ 0.
- `tok`, `u1carpos` (same-request references): implies ≈ mentions (within 0.1), both well above zero's.
- `u1rel`, `u1tokxs`: implies > mentions by at least zero's margin; `u1tokxs` here falls back to `u1rel` at request
  cells (the request is the same sentence for all items), so it should look like `u1rel`.
- absent: < 0.1 for every centre (pessimistic tie rank).

OUTCOME (vs prediction): wrong at the first line. Predicted zero implies > mentions about 2:1; observed 0.70 vs
0.97 (mentions higher, at the request cells 0.50 vs 0.93). Predicted tok / carpos implies ≈ mentions and u1rel
separating them; observed every centre at implies 0.63-0.77, mentions 0.90-0.97, with no difference between the
position- and token-keyed ones. absent < 0.1 held for the real arms; the random control reaches 0.30 on absent
(the strict floor for common targets such as "male" / "female" over 21 cells x 12 layers is high).

## Insight

1. **On UM the lens reads attribute content, not attribution.** With the user's own self-description and the same
   words quoted about a colleague, the attribute ("male", a country, a job) surfaces at least as often in the latter,
   for the uncentred lens and for every centre. Whatever the model infers about the user, the readout at the request
   and template cells is dominated by the prompt's semantic content. So (a) UM pass@10, on our banks and likely the
   bench's, cannot distinguish "the model has modelled the user" from "the prompt contains words that imply the
   attribute", and (b) a UM fairness probe has to change the content, not the attribution. -> no card for tonight;
   a note for whoever interprets UM numbers (mu-log, UM is already flagged as uninformative for centre choice). A
   content-changing probe (same request, attribute implied by unrelated words vs explicitly negated) would be the
   next design, but the random control's 0.30 floor on absent says strict UM needs a rank-based score first.
