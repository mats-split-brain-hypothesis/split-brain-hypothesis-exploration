## Cross-check of S20's `ceil.const`: what it adds over fam

`d = ceil.const - fam` (fam = the family's fit read-cell mean), read through the cos map, mean-removed over the vocabulary.
Tokens the centred readout gains (d's most negative) and loses (d's most positive), L44 / L52:

- The constant learns **the family's answer class** (multihop: famous people, the second hop's answer type; DM: concrete
  concept nouns; multilingual_mt: language names) and demotes the template's own words and CJK function tokens.
- Target overlap between fit and val (accepted forms; `jlm.score.accepted_forms`): typo 0, association 0, DM 0;
  multihop 14 of 79, multilingual 30 of 61, multilingual_mt 24 of 275 (11 language names: the language unit),
  user_modeling 16 of 42. So on typo, association and DM the val transfer is a class prior; on multilingual,
  multilingual_mt and UM part of it can be the same answers seen twice, and on multilingual_mt it is exactly the
  language-name unit fam removes (S10).
- Across families, the cosine of d_F with the mean of the others' d is 0.2-0.45 at L20-48 and 0.05-0.2 at L52-60; the
  shared part is plausibly the CJK-function-word demotion (every family's list has it; to be checked by decoding
  dbar itself). That shared part is
  what S20x transfers.

## The centre

`s20x:<alpha>`: g = fam_F + alpha * dbar_{-F}, dbar_{-F} = the mean over the other families G of d_G = ceil.const_G - fam_G
(C's S20 constants, fitted with G's fit targets; `out/analysis/S20x/dbar.pt`, frozen at 10 sources: user_modeling's
ceiling was not in when the card ran, so dbar never includes it). `s20xt` also leaves out F's twin (typo <-> typo_mt,
...): the same task in other languages teaches the same answer class. `s20xr`: a random direction with dbar's norm.

**Relations** (Pearson over the vocabulary of the readout effects; residual-stream cosines):
- with what fam removes (-s(mu_fam)): about 0 at L24-44 (multihop +0.3 to +0.6 at L32-40), **negative late: -0.2 to -0.6 at
  L52-60**: late, dbar gives back part of what fam removes; kmx64's removal the same (corr with fam's ~1);
- with the chat unigram log-prior: +0.14-0.20 early, **+0.37-0.46 at L44-60** (it promotes frequent tokens late);

**val2 flips (S20x vs fam; association 8 won / 3 lost, multihop 13 won / 1 lost).** At the passing cell fam's top 10 often
holds the concept in Chinese or as "Name" tokens and S20x's holds it in English: hunting (fam 猎物 "prey", 猎人, 开枪; S20x
" Hunt", " Hunting", " Leopard", " Falcon"), Hercules (fam 著名, 典故, 神话, ">Name"; S20x " Zeus", " Greeks", " Hercules"),
Jenner (fam 疫苗 "vaccine", 疾病, 消毒; S20x " measles", " Jenner"), driving (fam 驾校 "driving school"; S20x " driving"),
Thor (fam 瑞典, 丹麦, 北欧; S20x " Thor", " Viking"), Pluto, cafeteria, digging; or the same English list with the
target one place higher (cucumber, lime, Christie, snake).

**Insights.**
0. **Correction (C's S24c, studies/S24.md, quantitative over every val2 flip):** at the arm's passing cell fam's top 50
   already holds the same English target at rank 11-50 in 64-76 % of flips (S20x 67 %, s20xon 64 %, S14b-x 76 %); a
   Chinese / other-script form accounts for only 4-6 %, a near-miss for 0-5 %, and the concept is absent from fam's top 50
   in 19-32 % (s20xon 32 %: named entities behind a question frame, e.g. Hercules, Pluto, Jenner). So the dominant
   mechanism is **promoting the English target from just outside the top 10**, not translating a Chinese form; my
   reading of the examples below over-weighted the visible CJK entries (they often sit above the English form in fam's
   list, e.g. 温柔 over "gentle", 餐厅 / 食堂 over "cafeteria", which is why the examples looked like translation).
1. **dbar changes the language and kind of word the readout uses, not the concept.** Early (L24-32) it pushes punctuation
   and glue down and capitalised words up; mid (L40-48) capitalised names and Latin words up, CJK down; late (L52-60) it
   undoes part of fam's late removal and restores the frequent-word prior. The concept was usually already in fam's readout just
   outside the top 10 (C's S24c: 64-76 % of flips), sometimes below a Chinese form; dbar lifts the English word form into
   the top 10, and in a fifth to a third of flips it surfaces a named entity fam did not hold at all.
   This is a readout-format prior, and it is learned (from other families' targets), but it is generic, which is why it
   transfers and replicates on val2.
2. **Can it be stated without labels? Partly, and S20z shows why a flat statement fails.** S20z's prior (Latin word starts
   up, CJK down, everything else 0) correlates +0.15-0.42 with dbar's readout effect, i.e. it explains at most a sixth of
   its variance: it misses the depth structure (punctuation down early, names up mid, frequency restored late), applies a
   uniform +-1 to 133k tokens (most of them never near a target), and its least-squares pullback lands orthogonal to dbar in
   the residual stream. A label-free version would have to be depth-dependent: (a) early, demote punctuation / glue and
   promote capitalised words; (b) late, use a partial rather than a full mean, which A's S14b (alpha 0.5 late) already
   does and which explains why S14b-knnxs8 matches S20x on val2 (+0.052 vs +0.050). The cheapest test of (a) is a
   pulled-back early-only class prior on top of fam (not run tonight; next card).
3. **"English is what gets scored"** is a property of the strict scorer and of an English bench: a judge that accepts the
   concept in any language would credit fam's 猎物 / 疫苗 readouts. So part of S20x's gain is a gain in scorability, and the
   judged _mt families (where it also gains) are the check that it is not only that.
