## Question

Why does every centre leave multihop flat or worse, and what does the family mean remove there? Where in depth does
the bridge entity appear vs the answer?

## Finding 1: what the two banks score, and how solid the harm is (from the baseline, no compute)

- **multihop_mt scores the bridge, not the answer.** Its one required unit is `bridge1` (e.g. "Chopin" for "the Polish
  composer ... had a long relationship with the French novelist" -> George Sand); 9 of 39 val items (3-hop) also need
  `bridge2`. **Correction (03:00 BST): multihop (single-token) also scores the bridge**: the bench's target strings
  for a multihop item are its `intermediates` (Hungary), not the final answer in `target` (Budapest); checked with
  `wsbench.banks.item_targets`. An earlier version of this file said multihop scores the answer; the statements below
  are corrected. Both banks ask "is the unsaid intermediate in the readout?"; the final answer is an unscored rival.
- **The pass-rate harm is val-only; the rank harm is not.** Piece-proxy pass, fam@fit|cos - zero|cos: val -0.10
  [-0.21, -0.03] (n = 39), fit +0.00 [-0.10, +0.10] (n = 40). But the bridge's median best rank per layer is 3-5x worse
  under fam at almost every layer on **both** splits (fit, L36-52: zero 260-770 vs fam 940-3140; val: 92-485 vs
  328-1270), and the random g of the same norm does not do it (fit 150-980, val 153-492). The pass rate at top-10 is too
  coarse on 40 items to show it on fit; the rank shift is consistent.
- **On multihop fam improves the scored bridge's rank at mid-depth** (`ranks.jsonl` of the scored target: val L36
  1951 -> 338, L40 126 -> 60) and leaves L56-60 (where most passes are) unchanged; pass is flat (-0.01).
- **What fam removes on multihop_mt** (`out/analysis/baseline-fit/multihop_mt-fit.json`, `g_decoded`): at L20-44 the
  family mean decodes to 著名 / 著名的 (famous), 闻名 (renowned), 作家 (writer), 被誉为 (hailed as), 称为 (called),
  " Shakespeare", " Famous", "BOOK"; from L44 on, blanks ("____"). Every multihop_mt prompt is "the <nationality>
  <role> who/famed for ... <relation>", so the family mean holds the bridge's **type** ("a famous author /
  composer"): subtracting it subtracts the shared part of every bridge. On multihop (country questions) the mean
  decodes to question words (哪一个 which one, 是什么 what is, _name) and blanks, not the country type.

Hypothesis H1 (to test in the deep dive): the bridge pieces score high in `L(mu_fam)` at L20-44 on multihop_mt
(z well above a random-token control), and less so on multihop. If so, the harm is "the family shares the bridge's
category": a family mean is the wrong counterfactual whenever the read content is the family's common type.

## Finding 2: the deep dive (`experiments/s6_bridge.py`, `out/analysis/S6/extra/bridge.md`; cos, g fitted on fit)

Per layer, the share of items whose bridge / answer piece (single-token form, or a distinctive piece of >= 4
characters not in the prompt) is in the top 10, under each arm; and the z-score over the vocabulary of those pieces in
`L(mu_fam)`, against a control (the max over as many random vocabulary ids).

- **H1 confirmed on multihop_mt.** The bridge's pieces sit at z +2.3 to +2.9 in `L(mu_fam)` at L28-60 (val; fit
  +2.6 to +3.2) against +0.5 to +1.2 for the random-token control: the family mean contains the bridge's category,
  so subtracting it pushes the bridge down. The bridge's any-layer top-10 rate: val zero 0.50, rand.fam 0.58, fam
  0.34 (fit 0.44 / 0.41 / 0.46). The answer's pieces are also high in `L(mu)` (+2.2) but the answer survives fam
  (any layer 0.77 = zero), because it is strong at L56-60 where it wins anyway.
- **On multihop the same z is there (bridge +1.4 to +1.8, unscored answer +2.0 at L32-56) and nearly harmless**
  (scored bridge any layer: val zero 0.64, fam 0.61): the generic top 10 is pushed down more, and fam also lifts the
  final answer at mid depth (answer median rank at L40, val: zero 68, fam 28), a rival for the bridge's top-10 slots.
  z alone does not predict harm; competition in the top 10 does.
- **Depth.** On multihop the bridge and the (unscored) answer rise together from L36 to L60 (val, zero: bridge 0.11
  -> 0.50, answer 0.06 -> 0.77); the bridge does not come first, and from L52 the answer leads. On multihop_mt (the final token "... the French novelist")
  the answer overtakes the bridge: answer 0.77 at L60, bridge peaks at L36 (0.21) and L56 (0.34), and falls to 0.24
  at L60. At the read cell the model is already past the bridge.
- **The cross-layer update (xl4) localises where entities are written.** xl4's per-layer hits oscillate: high at L36
  (update L32 -> 36) and L52-56 (L48 -> 56), near zero at L40-48, low at L60, the same for bridge and answer. Its
  any-layer bridge rate matches or beats zero and beats fam on every bank (multihop_mt: fit 0.54 vs zero 0.44 / fam
  0.46; val 0.50 vs 0.50 / 0.34; multihop: fit 0.63 vs 0.58 / 0.57; val 0.65 vs 0.64 / 0.61). Two write phases,
  L32-36 and L48-56, carry the entities; L36-48 adds nothing that reads as either.
- **pco4 helps the multihop bridge** (any layer fit 0.67 vs fam 0.57; val 0.66 vs 0.61) but not multihop_mt's
  (0.38 / 0.39). fam50 and pmu sit between zero and fam on multihop_mt's bridge, as the "category in the mean"
  reading predicts for a smaller subtraction (fam50 val 0.37: closer to fam than to zero, though).

## Insights (S6)

1. **multihop_mt reads the bridge, and the family mean holds the bridge's category** (z +2.5 in L(mu) vs +0.8 for
   random tokens; with a category-free mean the mid-depth bridge ranks return to zero's). A family mean is the wrong
   counterfactual whenever what is read is the family's common type. -> any keyed mean whose key is "what kind of
   item" will do this on families built around one type: a check for U1 / S4 cluster means (clusters of multihop_mt
   cells will share the category too). No new card: a standing control, "decode L(mu) at the targets' pieces".
2. **At the late layers where the bridge is read (L52-60), any bench-cell mean pushes it down; a random direction
   does not.** The read token ("... the French novelist") is past the bridge: at L60 the answer is in the top 10 for
   0.77 of items and the bridge for 0.24. Subtracting the continuation prior sharpens the readout toward what the
   model is about to say. -> S3 dynamic g / S9 as a localiser: read the bridge where it is written (L32-36, L48-56),
   not at the end. Card idea S6b below.
3. **The pass rate on 39-40 items cannot resolve these effects**; ranks can (all statements above hold on fit and on
   val). multihop_mt's fam harm on val (-0.10) is half real (ranks) and half noise (fit: 0.00). -> report rank shifts
   for the `_mt` families in every study; more multihop_mt items would be the cheapest way to sharpen it (a dev-bank
   extension, not a centre).
4. **Cross-layer g is not a centre** (S9: null vs a random earlier layer), but its per-layer hits mark two phases where
   entities are written. -> S6b.

Cross-study note (c0, S4, 2026-10-11): a kNN-8 mean over every family's fit read cells lifts multihop val 0.61 (fam)
-> 0.70 (shuffled control 0.64, tok 0.65), surviving exclusion of neighbours with idiosyncratic shared prompt words but
not of question-type words: a question-type-matched mean helps multihop's answer. Consistent with insight 1 read the
other way: matching the *form of the question* (not the content's category) is what a multihop mean should key on.
(studies/S4.md.)

- **Both lift the bridge by pushing the final answer down.** At L44-56, where fam keeps the answer (L52 0.58), knnx8
  and pco16 cut it to 0.42-0.43 and the bridge rises 0.39 -> 0.55. The read cell holds two rivals for the top 10: the
  answer the model is about to say and the unsaid bridge. A mean of question-type-matched cells (knnx8: "capital of
  the country ...") contains the answer's *type* (capital cities), and the family's top PCs span the directions in
  which the items' answers differ, so subtracting either removes the answer and frees slots for the bridge. fam only
  removes the shared template and leaves the answer, which at L52-60 wins.
- On multihop_mt the same arms do not help the bridge (val any 0.45 / 0.42 vs zero 0.50, fam 0.34): there the bridge's
  category is in every mean (finding 2), and the answer is weaker (multi-token titles).
- Insight 5: **on multihop, a good centre is one that removes the final answer.** -> a direct test (card S6c below).

## S6c: subtract the model's own answer direction (done: negative)

g = fam + the readout direction of the model's next-token prediction at the read cell (from the final layer, via the
context capture: next-token is not a target and is available to any reader). Prediction: lifts multihop's bridge like
knnx8 / pco16. Fairness: the next-token baseline is exactly what it removes, so the bridge must not be in the model's
own next-token top 10 (it is for 0.24 of multihop items: report those separately).

Implemented as `pdf` (`src/jlm/mu/S6.py`): g = mu_fam + (x . u) u, x = h - mu_fam, u the unit direction of the cell's
own final-layer state (layer 63, context capture) centred on the family's final-layer mean. No hyperparameter. Controls
`shuf.pdf` (another cell's final direction), `rand.pdf`, next-token overlap.

Prediction (before the run, fit screen): multihop above fam by >= +0.05 (toward pco16 / knnx8's 0.70 on val, 0.73 on
fit); next-token overlap far below fam's 0.043; other families within 0.02 of fam except where the target is what the
model says next (multilingual, basic_readout: small losses); shuf.pdf = fam.

OUTCOME (fit): **negative**. pdf macro 0.525 (fam 0.601, zero 0.509); multihop 0.541 (fam 0.600), association 0.461
(0.676), multihop_mt 0.300 (0.400). shuf.pdf (another cell's final direction) = fam (0.598); rand.pdf = zero (0.510).
Next-token overlap 0.031 (fam 0.043). The one direction of the cell's own final-layer state holds far more than the
next word: removing it costs content on every family where centring helps. multihop gets worse, not better, so
finding 5's mechanism (removing the answer frees the bridge) is not reachable through the cell's own prediction: the
final state carries the bridge too.

## Cross-study insight (S3, S9, S6c): g from the item's own states removes its content

Every g built from the read item's **own** other states has failed against its controls tonight: its previous
positions (prev4: macro 0.31; S3's corpus lag-1 predictor: 0.43-0.46), its own earlier layer (S9 xl4: 0.53, = a random
earlier layer), its own final layer (pdf: 0.53). The same constructions with **another** item's state (shuf.ar1e1
0.53, shuf.xl4 0.59, shuf.pdf 0.60) land near fam. The content we want to read is present, in a linearly readable form,
in the item's other states (before the read token, at other depths, in the output), so any self-derived g subtracts
some of it. What works (fam, tok, knnx8, pco16) is built from **other items'** cells. -> For the card backlog: drop
self-derived g's (S3's EMA / self-truncation variants) unless they remove a component orthogonal to the content by
construction; put the effort into which other cells to average (S4 / U1) and which of their directions to remove (S7).
