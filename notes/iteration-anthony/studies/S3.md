## Centre

`ar1e<p>`: g = my + (h_{t-1} - mx) A, A the ridge regression of h_t on h_{t-1} at the same layer, fitted on the wikitext
context capture (256 windows x 128 tokens, positions >= 16, about 28.7k pairs per layer), lambda = 10^-p tr(C)/d,
p in {0, 1, 2}. h_{t-1} is the read cell's own previous position (the item's context capture). As lambda grows g tends
to the wikitext mean (G1).

Why this and not prev4: `prev4` (the item's own last 4 states) removed the item's content (multihop 0.11). A corpus
predictor only removes what *any* text with this previous state would have at t: the generic continuation of the
previous state. Whether that keeps the item's content is the question.

Fairness: the predictor never sees a bank item. Risk (category "functions of the context"): what is new at t is the
read token itself or the next-token prediction. Controls: next-token overlap (critical), `rand.ar1e1`, `shuf.ar1e1`,
echo; prompt-masked pass.

## Insight

1. **The previous state predicts the item's content, not only the generic continuation.** Harm grows monotonically
   with the predictor's strength (macro 0.462 -> 0.440 -> 0.425 as lambda falls) and is largest exactly where
   centring gains most (association 0.33 -> 0.16, multihop 0.61 -> 0.33), while a random g with the same norm is at
   zero's level and another cell's prediction (`shuf`) recovers part of fam's gain (0.527). By the read token the
   content (the misspelt word, the cue, the bridge) is already in h_{t-1}, and a linear map fitted on any text carries
   it forward. A dynamic g built from the item's own past positions removes what we want to read, with or without a
   corpus in between (prev4 is the extreme). -> A dynamic g must take its context from **other** items (shuf.ar1e1 is
   one: another cell's prediction), i.e. it collapses into keyed / neighbour means (U1, S4: c0's kNN over other
   families' cells lifts multihop 0.61 -> 0.70 on val). No new S3 card; S3's "self-truncation / EMA / linear predictor"
   family is closed as a centre.
