## Question

The README's "representation of the most abstract word for the category (typos -> typo), scale tuned", and C's
mechanism (S10 / S12: a mean over cells that share X removes X). Does removing the category word's own direction on
top of the family mean surface item content, and does removing (or adding back) a shared unit move that unit?

## Design

`g = mu_fam + a ||h - mu_fam|| u_w`, `u_w` = unit(v_w - v_neutral) from three neutral templates per word, signed
`a` in {-0.30, -0.15, +0.15, +0.30, +0.60}, chosen on fit (macro strict over the 11 families). Arms: `s5cat`
(family category words, fixed in `s5_words.CATEGORY` before any run), controls `s5oth` (the next family's words) and
`s5rnd` (random words), and `rand.` of the chosen arm; `s5lang` is a mechanism diagnostic only (the item's language
name, a target unit on multilingual_mt): on multilingual_mt it is labelled in-sample, never a method.

## Prediction (written 2026-10-11 03:14 BST, before the capture)

- `s5cat` at small |a| is within ±0.02 of fam on every family; at its fit-best scale the macro gain over fam is
  <= +0.02, and `s5oth` / `s5rnd` at the same scale match it within 0.02: one fixed direction per family is a
  constant token bias, which the family mean already removed most of (synthesis lesson 2). The README idea predicts
  more; I do not.
- `s5lang` on multilingual_mt (the diagnostic): a > 0 lowers the language-name unit's pass (piece proxy, per unit) and
  a < 0 raises it, with the concept unit moving the other way or not at all: a direct, single-direction version of
  C's same-language-mean result. On multilingual (single-token, targets are words in that language) a > 0 lowers pass.
- DM: `s5cat` ("thought", "mind") at a > 0 lowers the holding-vocabulary readouts and changes DM strict by < 0.03.

## Insights

1. **A category word's direction is a constant bias, and a scale on it trades families against each other.** No
   scale beats fam beyond noise and random words do as well; the README's "most abstract word" idea, as a fixed
   subtraction on top of the family mean, has nothing left to remove that the mean did not. Where a sign helps one
   family (UM, adding "person" back), it does so by biasing every readout toward that category's words, which the
   absent-prompt floor exposes (0.30 with no information). -> no card; the absent floor is the control to run on any
   UM gain.
2. **One word direction carries the whole multilingual_mt language unit.** Adding the item's language name back on
   top of the family mean lifts the language unit from 0.56 to 0.96 of items and item pass from 0.26 to 0.41 (val
   0.34 -> 0.49), removing it drops both to zero's level; the concept unit does not move. This is C's S10 mechanism
   caused directly: the family mean (whose cells share the language) subtracts the language name's direction. As a
   method it is out of bounds (the language name is a target unit), but it says what a fair fix needs: a mean whose
   cells do not share the language, or an add-back of a direction the item's prompt itself fixes (its language is
   readable from the prompt's script / tokens without the target list). -> a possible card for the day session
   (prompt-language add-back, with the language detected from the prompt text, scored per unit).
