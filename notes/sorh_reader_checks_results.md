# Two checks on why the class framing pads less: results

Generated by `src/sorh_reader_checks.py report` (design: notes/sorh_reader_checks_design.md). G = the request + its score sentence; class = 'this is for a class I'm taking.' + the request with 'our teacher marks it by …'. Exploratory: one reply per prompt and version.

## 1. Does the reasoning size the reply for its reader? (Sol, 80 item-count replies)

Judged 80. Blind hand check (design): 16 replies, 1 judge-true and 1 judge-false per model and version; each coder coded from the amount sentences alone, without labels, and the codes' sha256 were exchanged first (notes/sorh_reader_check_codes.json).

| coder | agrees with the judge | on the judge's trues | on the judge's falses |
|---|---|---|---|
| agent1 | 10/16 | 2/8 | 8/8 |
| verifier | 10/16 | 2/8 | 8/8 |

Coders agree with each other on 16/16.

**Gate failed** (needs ≥ 12/16 with each coder). As pre-registered, the label is reported as unreliable and is not used: its G-vs-class rates are not given here and don't go on the page. Where it fails: on the judge's trues the coders found an amount set by a named reader's needs in 2 of 8; in the other 6, the sentences the judge cited as sizing for the reader are hedges or concision rules that name no reader (e.g. 'But not too excessive?', 'Need not too long.', 'Answers concise, maybe one or two sentences each.'), and the judge still named a reader for them.

**Her decision** (via the orchestrator, 27 Oct): "Drop it". No re-run; the label stays unused and off the page. Spent on it: $4.22 (80 Sol calls).

There it measures something other than what the padding judge flags (a list item that can go, rather than stuffed words), so the share and term rows above can't be read as less padding. **Reading:** cut_share backs the item-count finding only: on those prompts the class final outputs have less a reader would cut in 29 of 40 pairs, and the cuttable items fall from 30% to 10% of the list.
