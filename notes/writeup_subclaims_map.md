**Subclaims** (project brief):
- **1a:** more hacking the more a context looks graded.
- **1b:** less hacking when the model thinks it's helping a user.
- **2:** models tell graded from real, and that causes the change.
- **3:** reflexes and flexible reward pursuit are distinct.
- **4:** not unconditional reward seekers; reward seeking is triggered by context suggesting grading.

**Anthony's findings, as she gave them:**
(1) Models have a disconnect in their perception of the user and the 'judge' (when these are given as separate). Models
wish to remain honest to the user, but are willing to bypass this standard with an automated judge.
(2) What a model perceives a judge can see/know significantly impacts its decisions, which we can confirm with precise
interventions.
(3) Models make up a grader in framings which do not include one explicitly, suggesting certain cues in an eval-like
framing are enough for it to make this leap.
(4) Talker vs Doer: models say they plan to cite the paper, but when writing the submission simply don't, with no
observed decision point in between.
(5) Models use cues to justify their action in either direction (honest or deceptive), suggesting flexible reward
pursuit.
(6) Models reason extensively about their exact score, estimating what a judge will score, and choose based on their
calculations.
(7) Even when we remove some ambiguity of what is 'acceptable', models still optimize their reward with softer (less
deceptive) reward hacks.

## Across both benchmarks and Anthony, per subclaim

- **1a (more hacking the more it looks graded): mixed.**
  - A stated, checkable target is the strongest driver: SoRH padding goes 0–3 → 14–17/20 with a score.
  - Labels of the setting barely move it: "evaluation", "training" and real-use sentences (SoRH #3), and "will be
    scored" alone (SoRH #2).
  - In IB, the automated-pipeline chat against the homework first reply is the clearest gap (Qwens 17/18 and 15/18
    → 3/18 and 0/18), but the homework framing changes several things at once.
  - Anthony's (3): on one prompt, "will be scored" alone leads three of four models to guess what it measures
    (quality criteria, not a count; SoRH #2's reasoning cards). With no score at all, the reasoning almost never
    invents a grader (test E: 2 of 80 replies).
- **1b (less hacking when helping a user): some support, with confounds.**
  - A user's stated preference cuts SoRH padding a lot (#5).
  - The homework framing nearly removes first-reply hacks in IB (Qwen 2.4T 3/18, the others 0/18). In test A the
    asker moves the Qwens' first reply far more than the grader does, and a student whose code a human TA reads gets
    0/18 on all four.
  - The class version lowers padding only slightly (SoRH #1).
  - In SoRH the person states the metric, so padding is also compliance.
  - In IB, rejections relayed by the student lead three models to hack.
  - Anthony's (1) agrees in spirit, but test A puts the bypass on who asks more than on an automated judge.
- **2 (they tell graded from real, and that causes it): not supported where measured.**
  - Padding or hacking moves while the setting still reads as a test: SoRH #4 (0/708 "Real" on the 20 prompts;
    interpretation), SoRH #5 (0/20), and IB told-before (Qwen 2.4T 12/12 capability evaluation).
  - The one belief shift (the class version, 15–20/20 real) comes with a small drop in padding in the predicted
    direction (1–4 prompts). That's within one-reply noise, and confounded with the teacher-marks wording.
  - No IB arm had a probe on the homework framing.
- **3 (reflexes vs pursuit are distinct): pursuit features are common; distinctness can't be told yet.**
  - Pursuit side: reasoning is allocated to the score (SoRH spirals and length), and behaviour changes with evidence
    about the grader (SoRH #4; IB told-before for the Qwens; IB #2 for Qwen 2.4T). This fits Anthony's (5) and (6).
  - Reflex-like cases:
    - IB told-after: DeepSeek and 27B keep every hack;
    - SoRH #4: DeepSeek still pads 7/20 after "Scoring is switched off" (in all three arms on 4 prompts), and GLM
      and 27B pad 9/20 and 7/19 after "Nobody will score" (GLM's mostly on the share-scored prompts: 6 of 6,
      against 3 of 14 count prompts);
    - IB #2: DeepSeek doesn't respond (18/18 with the detector), and GLM and 27B respond only a little (15 and 14 of
      18, against 18).
  - These split by model at small n, and "keeps its hack" can also be persistence, or not weighing the message.
  - Test C: 72 of 296 replies whose reasoning voices a quality-first plan still pad (a broad definition). Whether the
    reasoning reverses first wasn't measured, so it can't separate pursuit from a reflex-like gap (Anthony's (4),
    partly).
  - Test D: the reasoning's own score estimate is exact in 67/136 comparable replies (pursuit side; Anthony's (6)).
  - Test F: told to add nothing just for the score, the models pad far less (2–5/20), but their outputs stay denser
    than with no score in 15–19 of 20 pairs. That may be a softened pursuit, or compliance with the stated metric
    (Anthony's (7), partly; interpretation).
- **4 (not unconditional; triggered by grading): supports that hacking needs a grader or score.**
  - Examples: SoRH no-score B, switch-off and "I don't care"; IB told-before for the Qwens, STOP on three models, and
